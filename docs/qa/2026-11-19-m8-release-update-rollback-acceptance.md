## Historical 2026-11-20 restricted-worker handoff (superseded by the verification above)

- **Source changes:** Recovery/quarantine copies refuse unapproved linked ancestors and compare opened directory identities with the lstat snapshots recorded before opening. On Linux, each directory component is opened relative to its pinned parent through `/proc/self/fd` with no-follow flags; source and destination leaves are then opened relative to the pinned final directories. On macOS, the root-owned `/var` alias is accepted only after resolving it to `/private/var`; other linked ancestors remain rejected. macOS/Windows do not use descriptor-relative `openat`, so same-user concurrent rename races remain an explicit platform limitation. One family invocation allows at most **512 MiB per file, 1 GiB total and a shared 10-second monotonic budget**. Copying is synchronous on the caller, including Electron main; elapsed time is checked around chunk reads and writes and after `fsync`, so a slow system call can exceed the budget and delay the caller. The free-space check does not account for quotas, and a failed copy may leave partial bytes in its private staging directory; production callers remove that directory on failure. The previous macOS `cp -c` optimization remains retired pending descriptor-safe native implementation and macOS acceptance.
